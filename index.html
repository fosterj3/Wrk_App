What it does

One log for all of it

Weight and reps, distance and time, a held plank, or just "45 minutes of pilates". Lifting, running, cycling, yoga — it all lands in the same history instead of three different apps.

Forgot to log it? Two taps.

Tap any past day in the calendar and fill it in — including the time you actually trained, not the time you finally remembered. Missing the logging shouldn't mean missing the record.

Never guess what you did last time

Every exercise shows your last session right above the boxes you're filling in, so you know the number to beat before you start. One tap copies it across.

Not sure where to start?

Answer five questions and get a real week built for you — lifting, cardio, yoga or a mix — with notes on how to actually run it, not just a list of exercises.

Charts that answer real questions

Am I training as often as I think? Is it getting heavier? What time of day do I actually show up? A handful of answers rather than a wall of numbers.

Timers that match the work

Rest counts down between sets, loud enough to hear over music. For a plank, a 10k or a yoga class, the stopwatch counts up and writes the time straight in.

No account. Nothing to sign up for.

No email, no password, no free trial that turns into a subscription. Open it and start logging. Everything you record lives in your own browser storage, so nobody — including me — can see your training, because there is nowhere for it to go.

The trade is that you are the backup. Export a file whenever you like: a restoreable backup, or a spreadsheet you can open anywhere or email to a coach.

Open it, then:

Android — Chrome

  1. Tap the three-dot menu ⋮ at the top right.
  2. Tap Install and add shortcut. Older versions of Chrome call this Add to Home screen.
  3. Confirm Install.

iPhone — Safari only

  1. Tap the Share icon. On newer iOS it sits behind the three-dot button ⋯ beside the address bar.
  2. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  3. Leave Open as Web App switched on, then tap Add.

Chrome, Firefox and Edge on iPhone cannot do this — only Safari can. And with that toggle off you get a plain bookmark rather than an app: no full screen, no offline.

Installed, it opens full screen and runs without a signal — useful in a basement gym.
