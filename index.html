What it does

Knows what you lifted last time

Every exercise shows your last session's sets, so you walk up to the bar already knowing the number to beat. One tap copies it in.

A calendar you can fill in late

Colour-coded by lifting, cardio or both. Forgot to log Tuesday? Tap Tuesday and add it.

Charts that answer real questions

Am I training as often as I think? Is my bench actually going up? Estimated 1RM per exercise, volume per week, and how often you showed up.

Timers for rest and for holds

Rest counts down between sets. For a plank, the stopwatch counts up and writes the time straight into the set.

A weekly goal that nudges

Set a target. If it's Thursday and you're one of three, Cadence says so — while there's still time to fix it.

Personal records, called out

Beat your best estimated 1RM on a lift and it tells you, right when it happens.

Your data stays on your phone

There is no account and no server. Everything you log lives in your own browser storage, which means nobody — including me — can see your training. It also means you are the backup: export a file whenever you like, as a restoreable backup or as a spreadsheet you can email to a coach.

Put it on your home screen

Cadence is a web app, so there's no app store and nothing to update. Open it, then:

Android — Chrome

  1. Tap the three-dot menu ⋮ at the top right.
  2. Tap Install and add shortcut. Older versions of Chrome call this Add to Home screen.
  3. Confirm Install.

iPhone — Safari

  1. Tap the Share icon. On newer iOS it sits behind the three-dot button ⋯ beside the address bar.
  2. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  3. Leave Open as Web App switched on, then tap Add.

It has to be Safari — and with that toggle off you get a plain bookmark instead of an app, so no full screen and no offline.

Installed, it opens full screen and runs without a signal — useful in a basement gym.
